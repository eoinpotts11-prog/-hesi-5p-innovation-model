# Sustainable Development Goal Alignment Guide

**Applying the HESI–5P Innovation Model to all 17 United Nations Sustainable Development Goals through evidence, human accountability and the commitment to leave no one behind.**

> Working draft 0.1 · August 2026 · Project author: Eoin Potts

## Purpose

This guide helps projects assess whether an innovation could make a credible contribution to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). It connects the HESI principles—Human-centred, Evidence-led, Sustainable and Inclusive—with the five-P structure of People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace and Partnership.

An SDG icon, label or statement of intent is not evidence of impact. A credible claim should identify the relevant need, affected people, baseline, intended outcome, indicators, risks, trade-offs, accountable owner and review process.

## Core alignment rules

1. **Leave no one behind.** Identify who is underserved, excluded, disproportionately burdened or unable to participate, and design with those groups rather than only for them.
2. **Measure outcomes, not activity alone.** Training sessions, funding spent or systems installed may be useful outputs, but they do not prove improved lives or environmental conditions.
3. **Do not hide trade-offs.** A benefit under one goal cannot cancel an unmanaged serious harm under another goal, to human rights or to an affected community.
4. **Use the full life cycle.** Include supply chains, energy, water, materials, land, labour, operation, maintenance, decommissioning, waste and possible rebound effects.
5. **Compare credible alternatives.** Assess the proposed innovation against current practice, a strong non-technical or classical alternative, and the option of taking no action.
6. **Keep people accountable.** AI, analytics, simulation and other technologies may support assessment, but a named person or body must remain responsible for decisions and consequences.
7. **Report uncertainty and negative findings.** Unknown, adverse and inconclusive results are decision-relevant evidence and should not be suppressed.
8. **Review and adapt.** Set review dates and conditions for revision, pause or termination when evidence, circumstances or impacts change.

## Five-P map

The SDGs are integrated and indivisible. The grouping below is a practical starting point, not a set of silos; most real projects affect several pillars and goals.

| Five-P pillar | Primary goals | HESI–5P question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **People** | SDGs 1–5 | Does the project protect dignity, meet essential needs and improve health, education and equality? |
| **Planet** | SDGs 6 and 12–15 | Does it protect climate, water, land, resources and ecosystems across the full life cycle? |
| **Prosperity** | SDGs 7–11 | Does it create fair, resilient and durable social and economic value? |
| **Peace** | SDG 16 | Does it strengthen rights, justice, safety, accountability and trustworthy institutions? |
| **Partnership** | SDG 17 | Are knowledge, power, responsibility, finance and benefits shared fairly? |

## HESI quality gates for SDG claims

| Principle | Required test | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Human-centred** | Have affected people shaped the problem, options, safeguards and success measures? | Co-design record, accessible engagement, consent where applicable, complaints and redress route |
| **Evidence-led** | Is the claimed contribution supported by a baseline, transparent method and independently examinable results? | Data provenance, comparison method, uncertainty, disaggregated results and evaluation |
| **Sustainable** | Are environmental, social and economic effects assessed together over the full life cycle? | Energy, water, carbon, materials, waste, biodiversity, labour and affordability evidence |
| **Inclusive** | Can diverse groups participate, access the benefit and challenge decisions without disproportionate barriers? | Accessibility testing, representation, outcome-gap analysis, language access and fair-benefit measures |

## All 17 goals: contribution questions and evidence

The measures below are illustrative project indicators, not replacements for the official UN global indicator framework. Select a small number that are material to the decision and define the population, geography, units and time period.

| SDG | Goal | HESI–5P contribution question | Example project evidence or indicators |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | No Poverty | Will the project reduce material hardship or improve fair access to essential services without creating unaffordable costs or insecure dependency? | People reached below an agreed poverty threshold; household cost change; benefit take-up; access barriers; distribution of gains |
| **2** | Zero Hunger | Will it improve food security, nutrition and sustainable agriculture while protecting land, water, livelihoods and local food systems? | Food-insecurity change; nutritional outcomes; food loss and waste; soil and water effects; small-producer participation and income |
| **3** | Good Health and Well-being | Will it improve prevention, safety, care or wellbeing, particularly for underserved groups, with appropriate clinical and human oversight? | Health outcomes; access and waiting time; adverse events; prevention uptake; accessibility; outcome gaps between groups |
| **4** | Quality Education | Will it expand inclusive, accessible and effective learning and skills rather than widen digital or economic divides? | Participation and completion; learning outcomes; accessibility conformance; device or connectivity barriers; skills retained and applied |
| **5** | Gender Equality | Will it reduce gender-based discrimination, violence and unequal power, opportunity, pay or unpaid-care burdens? | Representation in design and leadership; pay and progression gaps; safety incidents; outcome differences; care-time effects |
| **6** | Clean Water and Sanitation | Will it protect water quality, availability, sanitation and equitable access across the supply chain and operating life? | Water withdrawal and consumption; reuse; pollution prevented; sanitation access; watershed impact; water-stress context |
| **7** | Affordable and Clean Energy | Will it improve energy access, affordability, efficiency or renewable supply without shifting environmental or social harm? | Energy intensity per useful outcome; renewable share; affordability; reliability; life-cycle emissions; community benefit |
| **8** | Decent Work and Economic Growth | Will it create safe, fairly paid and meaningful work, skills and productivity while respecting labour rights and avoiding harmful displacement? | Decent jobs created or sustained; pay; safety; training; worker voice; job displacement and transition support; local economic value |
| **9** | Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure | Will it strengthen inclusive, resilient infrastructure and responsible innovation that is accessible to communities and smaller organisations? | Reliability and resilience; accessibility; research value; local and SME participation; lifecycle impacts; affordability and reach |
| **10** | Reduced Inequalities | Will benefits, opportunities, risks and decision-making power be distributed fairly across income, disability, age, ethnicity, migration status, geography and other relevant characteristics? | Disaggregated outcome gaps; accessibility barriers; affordability; representation; complaints; remedies; benefit distribution |
| **11** | Sustainable Cities and Communities | Will it make settlements more inclusive, safe, accessible, resilient and environmentally sustainable? | Housing or service affordability; accessible mobility; travel time; air quality; disaster resilience; public-space and heritage effects |
| **12** | Responsible Consumption and Production | Will it reduce resource use, pollution and waste through prevention, durability, repair, reuse, circularity and responsible procurement? | Material footprint; product life; repairability; recycled and recovered content; waste and e-waste; supplier standards; toxic releases |
| **13** | Climate Action | Will it deliver credible mitigation or adaptation, improve resilience and avoid locking in emissions or vulnerability? | Absolute and lifecycle emissions; avoided emissions with baseline; adaptation coverage; resilience measures; transition risks; rebound effects |
| **14** | Life Below Water | Will it prevent marine pollution and protect oceans, coasts, aquatic biodiversity and sustainable livelihoods? | Runoff and discharge; plastics prevented; habitat condition; biodiversity effects; coastal-community participation; sustainable sourcing |
| **15** | Life on Land | Will it protect and restore terrestrial and freshwater ecosystems, soils, forests and biodiversity rather than displace damage elsewhere? | Habitat affected and restored; species and ecosystem condition; soil health; deforestation or conversion; invasive-species risk; monitoring duration |
| **16** | Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions | Will it strengthen rights, safety, transparency, privacy, security, accountability, access to justice and public trust? | Published decision records; audits; security and privacy incidents; complaints and appeals; response times; effective remedies; corruption controls |
| **17** | Partnerships for the Goals | Are affected communities and partners sharing knowledge, capability, responsibility and benefits through fair and transparent arrangements? | Partner diversity; co-designed changes; local leadership; shared standards and data; knowledge transfer; finance transparency; independent evaluation |

## Minimum evidence standard for an SDG claim

Before describing a project as aligned with or contributing to an SDG, record:

- the specific goal and relevant target or outcome;
- the affected population, place, ecosystem and time horizon;
- the baseline and credible comparison options;
- the causal explanation for how the activity is expected to create the outcome;
- a small set of indicators with units, sources, targets and review dates;
- results disaggregated where lawful, safe and necessary to detect unequal effects;
- risks, possible adverse impacts and interactions with other goals;
- safeguards, mitigation, redress and explicit stop conditions;
- the named accountable decision-maker or governing body; and
- the method for independent scrutiny, learning and publication of results.

## Applying the guide to the initial case study

The [AI + Quantum Computing Working Together](case-studies/ai-quantum-computing.md) case study identifies potential links to SDGs 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 16 and 17. These are hypotheses for testing rather than guaranteed contributions. Any pilot should select only the goals material to its intended use, establish a strong classical baseline and measure the complete technical, social, environmental and economic system boundary.

## Responsible use

This guide is a concept-stage governance aid. It is not UN endorsement, certification or proof of sustainability, and it does not replace the official SDG targets and indicators or specialist legal, equality, scientific, environmental, economic and human-rights assessment.
