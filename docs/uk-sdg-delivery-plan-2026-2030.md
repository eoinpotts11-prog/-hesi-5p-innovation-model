# UK Sustainable Development Goals Delivery Plan 2026–2030

**Proposed policy framework: one national architecture with four-nation and locally led delivery.**

> A practical system for turning all 17 Sustainable Development Goals into shared outcomes, funded delivery, transparent data and measurable improvement across the United Kingdom.

**People · Planet · Prosperity · Peace · Partnership**

> Working draft 0.1 · 14 August 2026 · Adapted for the HESI–5P Innovation Model repository

This is a proposal for public and parliamentary discussion. It is **not an official UK Government publication**, adopted policy, legal advice or a statement on behalf of any UK administration.

## Executive summary

> **CORE PROPOSITION** The United Kingdom should adopt one accountable SDG delivery system without imposing one uniform policy model. The centre sets common outcomes, evidence standards and reporting dates; each nation and locality chooses the most appropriate policies within its powers and democratic mandate.

The UK has strong institutions, research capacity and public services, but it is unlikely to complete every Sustainable Development Goal by 2030 at the current pace. The 2026 Sustainable Development Report ranks the UK eighth of 169 countries, while also identifying a much weaker international spillover performance. Domestic evidence shows persistent child poverty and health inequalities, incomplete climate delivery, and environmental targets that remain largely off track. [4]–[9]

This plan is designed to close the gap between commitment and implementation. It provides a single results framework for the 17 Goals, connects spending to outcomes, protects devolution, strengthens local decision-making, requires an annual report to the UK Parliament and establishes independent evaluation.

### The five delivery commitments

1.  Leave no one behind: measure who benefits, who is excluded and which places are falling furthest behind.

2.  Respect devolution and subsidiarity: decisions should be taken at the lowest effective democratic level.

3.  Align money with outcomes: Budgets, Spending Reviews, business cases and procurement should show their contribution and trade-offs.

4.  Report honestly every year: publish comparable data, explain red and amber results, and provide parliamentary time for scrutiny.

5.  Learn and adapt: evaluate implementation and impact independently, publish findings, scale what works and stop or redesign what does not.

### What adoption would establish

| **Element**                  | **Plan commitment**                                                                                                   |
|------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Political leadership         | A Cabinet Committee for Sustainable Development and an annual ministerial statement to Parliament.                    |
| Four-nation cooperation      | A UK SDG Council operating by consent on devolved matters and recognising existing national wellbeing frameworks.     |
| Local flexibility            | Voluntary, funded Local SDG Outcome Agreements co-designed with communities and aligned to local strategies.          |
| Funding discipline           | An SDG Budget Statement and Green Book-based appraisal of social, economic, environmental and distributional effects. |
| Public evidence              | A restored, accessible UK SDG dashboard with nation, regional, local and equality breakdowns where data permit.       |
| Parliamentary accountability | A statutory annual progress and delivery report laid by 31 July, followed by scrutiny and a government response.      |
| Independent evaluation       | A new UK Sustainable Development Evaluation Commission, coordinated with existing audit and expert bodies.            |
| Long-term continuity         | A 2028 mid-term evaluation, a 2030 national review and a 2031 final evaluation feeding the post-2030 framework.       |

### Plan status

This is a proposed policy framework, not an official UK Government publication. It is intended to be capable of adoption through a UK command paper, an intergovernmental agreement and a focused Sustainable Development Accountability Bill. New duties affecting devolved institutions would require consent; stronger existing duties, including those in Wales, would be preserved.

## 1. Mandate, scope and design principles

### 1.1 Mission

By 2030, the United Kingdom will make measurable progress across all 17 Sustainable Development Goals, prioritise people and places furthest behind, restore the natural systems on which wellbeing depends, and strengthen the institutions and partnerships needed for progress beyond 2030.

### 1.2 Scope

- Geography: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, with arrangements for reserved, devolved and local responsibilities.

- Institutions: UK departments, agencies, public bodies and major programmes; participation by devolved and local institutions through legislation, consent or outcome agreements.

- Time horizon: accelerated delivery to 2030, final evaluation in 2031, and transition to a post-2030 framework aligned with climate, nature and wellbeing commitments to 2050.

- Policy coverage: social, economic, environmental, institutional and international impacts, including effects embedded in UK consumption, finance and supply chains.

### 1.3 Design principles

| **Principle**              | **Operational meaning**                                                                            |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Universality               | All 17 Goals apply domestically as well as internationally.                                        |
| Leave no one behind        | National averages never substitute for distributional evidence.                                    |
| Subsidiarity               | Power and resources sit at the lowest level able to deliver the outcome effectively.               |
| Devolution by design       | The plan aligns frameworks; it does not recentralise devolved policy.                              |
| Prevention                 | Investment should reduce future harm and demand, not only treat failure after it occurs.           |
| Policy coherence           | Trade-offs and spillovers across goals, places and generations must be stated.                     |
| Additionality              | SDG labels must show improved decisions or outcomes, not rebrand business as usual.                |
| Open evidence              | Data, evaluation protocols, findings and government responses are published by default.            |
| Just transition            | Climate and industrial change must improve affordability, access, skills and regional opportunity. |
| Intergenerational fairness | Appraisal must identify significant costs or risks transferred to future generations.              |

### 1.4 Legal and policy route

Adoption should use three mutually reinforcing instruments: a command paper setting the policy framework; an intergovernmental agreement defining four-nation cooperation; and a focused UK Sustainable Development Accountability Act applying to UK institutions and reporting. Devolved legislatures may adopt equivalent or stronger arrangements. The Act should not amend devolved competence or reduce existing statutory protections.

> **NON-REGRESSION** Nothing in the UK framework should weaken the Well-being of Future Generations (Wales) Act 2015, Scotland's National Performance Framework, Northern Ireland's Programme for Government Wellbeing Framework, statutory climate and environment duties, equality law or human-rights obligations. [14]–[16]

## 2. Five national outcomes and the 17 Goals

The plan organises the 17 Goals under the UN's five-part logic of People, Planet, Prosperity, Peace and Partnership. This makes the framework easier to govern without treating interconnected goals as separate projects. [1]

| **Pillar**  | **Primary SDGs**        | **2030 outcome**                                                                                       |
|-------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| People      | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10    | Everyone can live with dignity, security, good health, learning and equal opportunity.                 |
| Planet      | 6, 7, 12, 13, 14 and 15 | The UK lives within environmental limits, restores nature and is resilient to climate risk.            |
| Prosperity  | 8, 9 and 11             | A productive, inclusive, low-carbon economy supports decent work and connected communities.            |
| Peace       | 16                      | Rights, safety, justice, trust and accountable institutions enable people and communities to flourish. |
| Partnership | 17 and cross-cutting    | Governments, places, sectors and countries combine knowledge, finance and delivery capacity.           |

### 2.1 People

Outcome: poverty and preventable ill health fall, essential services are accessible, and inequalities narrow between income groups, disabled and non-disabled people, ethnic groups, genders, generations and places.

- Reduce child poverty, deep material deprivation, food insecurity, homelessness and unaffordable housing costs.

- Shift health systems towards prevention and establish a measurable 2030 milestone for narrowing the healthy-life-expectancy gap.

- Close disadvantage gaps in early years, education, skills, employment and digital participation.

- Design public services and infrastructure accessibly, with reasonable adjustments and lived-experience testing built in.

- Assess every major policy for distributional effects and publish who gains, who pays and who may be excluded.

### 2.2 Planet

Outcome: clean energy, climate resilience, healthy water, a circular economy and recovering ecosystems are treated as foundations for health, security and long-term prosperity.

- Deliver clean power by 2030 and remain on the pathway for carbon budgets, the UK nationally determined contribution and net zero.

- Accelerate electricity networks, storage, home efficiency, low-carbon heat, public transport and industrial electrification.

- Move from waste management to prevention, repair, reuse and lower material footprints across government and the wider economy.

- Restore water, soils, species and effectively managed protected areas on land and at sea.

- Fund adaptation for heat, flooding, drought, coastal change, infrastructure failure and climate-related health risks.

### 2.3 Prosperity

Outcome: productivity and innovation improve alongside job quality, affordability, regional opportunity, resilient infrastructure and thriving communities.

- Create good jobs and apprenticeships in clean energy, rail, retrofit, construction, nature recovery, health technology and responsible AI.

- Connect investment in housing, transport, digital networks and public services to long-term local economic plans.

- Measure the quality, security, accessibility and pay of work, not employment totals alone.

- Use research, data and AI to augment public-service workers, with human oversight, accessibility and measurable environmental impact.

- Prioritise affordable, safe, low-carbon homes and 20-minute access to essential services wherever geography permits.

### 2.4 Peace

Outcome: people can exercise their rights, obtain justice, participate in decisions and trust that institutions are competent, honest, inclusive and accountable.

- Improve access to justice, administrative fairness, transparency and redress.

- Tackle hate crime, discrimination, violence, exploitation and online harms while protecting lawful expression and privacy.

- Strengthen public-sector capability, ethical standards, data stewardship and democratic resilience.

- Publish major delivery failures and lessons, including corrective action and named accountable owners.

### 2.5 Partnership

Outcome: the UK combines national capacity with devolved, local, civil-society, business, academic and international partnerships, with partners shaping outcomes rather than merely delivering centrally designed projects.

- Use joint outcome agreements and pooled budgets where problems cross institutional boundaries.

- Measure international spillovers from trade, finance, consumption, emissions and supply chains.

- Build reciprocal international partnerships based on locally led priorities, learning and long-term capacity.

- Share evidence openly across the four nations and with cities, regions and international partners.

## 3. Governance and accountability architecture

> **GOVERNANCE TEST** Every outcome must have a political owner, a senior responsible official, a funded delivery route, public indicators, an evaluation plan and a forum capable of resolving cross-government barriers.

| **Body**                                         | **Core role**                                                                                                                                              |
|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| UK Parliament                                    | Receives the statutory annual report; scrutinises progress, spending and evaluation; holds the UK Government to account.                                   |
| Cabinet Committee for Sustainable Development    | Sets UK priorities, resolves cross-departmental trade-offs and approves the annual report and corrective-action plan.                                      |
| Cabinet Office-HM Treasury SDG Secretariat       | Runs the delivery system, integrates outcomes with planning and spending, maintains the risk register and coordinates reporting.                           |
| UK SDG Council                                   | Quarterly four-nation ministerial forum; agrees shared definitions, cross-border priorities and voluntary nation annexes by consensus.                     |
| Local Delivery Forum                             | Mayors, combined and local authorities, local-government associations and community-planning representatives advise the Council and co-design local tools. |
| Lead portfolios and delivery boards              | Own pillar outcomes, maintain benefits maps and commission delivery and evaluation across departments and agencies.                                        |
| UK Sustainable Development Evaluation Commission | Publishes independent annual assessments, thematic evaluations, a 2028 mid-term review and a 2031 final evaluation.                                        |
| Official statistics system                       | ONS and devolved statistical authorities publish comparable, quality-assured and appropriately disaggregated data.                                         |

### 3.1 Cabinet Committee

The Prime Minister should chair the committee or formally delegate the role to a senior Cabinet minister. Permanent members should include the Chancellor and ministers responsible for devolution, local government, social security, health, education, business, transport, housing, environment, energy, justice, equalities and international development.

- Meet at least quarterly and publish a short communique after each meeting, except for legitimately confidential material.

- Review red-rated outcomes, cross-goal trade-offs and delivery barriers at each meeting.

- Approve corrective action, with owners, milestones and funding implications, for every red-rated national outcome.

- Review major international and domestic spillovers, including climate, nature, labour-rights and supply-chain risks.

### 3.2 UK SDG Council

The Council should include one lead minister from each administration. Its chair should rotate annually. Decisions affecting devolved responsibilities should be reached by consensus and recorded transparently. The Council cannot direct devolved institutions; nor can it prevent the UK Government acting within reserved responsibilities.

### 3.3 Accountability chain

| **Level**                | **Named accountability**                         | **Minimum responsibility**                                 |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Outcome owner            | Cabinet minister or devolved minister            | Outcome trajectory and cross-portfolio decisions           |
| Senior responsible owner | Named senior official                            | Integrated delivery plan, risks, benefits and data quality |
| Programme owner          | Department, agency or partnership                | Outputs, milestones, safeguards and evaluation readiness   |
| Place owner              | Mayor, council or community-planning partnership | Locally agreed outcomes and public engagement              |
| Independent assessor     | UKSDEC and relevant audit/expert bodies          | Credibility, impact, value for money and learning          |

## 4. Devolved and local flexibility

The UK framework should be a common architecture, not a uniform service model. It must align with the democratic, legal and statistical arrangements of each nation and with the varied needs of cities, towns, rural areas, coastal communities and islands.

| **Layer**                      | **What is shared or flexible**                                                                                                           |
|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| UK common floor                | Five outcomes; headline definitions; annual timetable; minimum equality breakdowns; open-data and evaluation standards.                  |
| Nation delivery agreements     | Each administration maps its own framework, targets and programmes to the five outcomes and identifies cross-border dependencies.        |
| Local SDG Outcome Agreements   | Participating authorities select local priorities, targets and delivery partners through public engagement and existing strategic plans. |
| Community and project charters | Funded programmes state intended outcomes, safeguards, local benefits, evaluation arrangements and routes for feedback or redress.       |

### 4.1 Four-nation alignment

| **Nation**       | **Primary alignment route**                                                                                                                                 |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| England          | UK missions, departmental plans, combined-authority strategies, local plans and voluntary Local SDG Outcome Agreements.                                     |
| Scotland         | National Performance Framework and National Outcomes, already aligned with the SDGs; Scottish targets and reporting remain nationally determined. [15]    |
| Wales            | Seven wellbeing goals, national indicators and public-body duties under the Well-being of Future Generations framework; non-regression is mandatory. [14] |
| Northern Ireland | Programme for Government 2024-2027 and its Wellbeing Framework, with equality, rural-needs and children's-rights assessment. [16]                         |

### 4.2 Local SDG Outcome Agreements

Agreements should be voluntary unless a legislature decides otherwise, last three to five years, and be integrated with existing local plans rather than creating a parallel strategy. Councils should not be required to prioritise all 17 Goals equally; they should explain their chosen priorities, dependencies and contribution to the common outcomes. [17]

- Co-design priorities with residents, charities, public services, employers, trade unions and education providers.

- Identify a small set of local outcome indicators plus relevant UK and nation indicators.

- Publish delivery partners, pooled or aligned funding, risks, milestones and evaluation questions.

- Report progress in accessible formats at least annually and hold a public scrutiny session.

- Use improvement support before sanctions; protect authorities facing high need or limited analytical capacity.

> **FUNDING GUARANTEE** No new local reporting or delivery duty should be imposed without a published funding and capacity assessment. The centre should reuse existing datasets and reporting wherever possible and provide multi-year analytical support.

## 5. Finance, appraisal and commissioning

The plan does not assume that relabelling existing spending will deliver the SDGs. It requires government to show how spending choices change outcomes, address trade-offs and create long-term public value. Appraisal and evaluation should use the 2026 Green Book, Magenta Book and relevant natural-capital and distributional guidance. [18]–[20]

### 5.1 SDG Budget Statement

HM Treasury should publish an SDG Budget Statement alongside each Budget and Spending Review. It should cover major tax and spending decisions, distributional effects, climate and nature impacts, place impacts, long-term liabilities and significant positive or negative international spillovers.

| **Budget test** | **Required question**                                                                               |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strategic fit   | Which outcome and target does the measure advance, and what problem evidence supports intervention? |
| Distribution    | Which income groups, protected groups, generations and places gain or bear costs?                   |
| Environment     | What are the lifecycle effects on emissions, resilience, air, water, nature and material use?       |
| Economic value  | What productivity, labour-market, infrastructure and local-growth outcomes are expected?            |
| Delivery        | Are capacity, skills, supply chains, dependencies and local implementation realistic?               |
| Evaluation      | What counterfactual, baseline, data, process, impact and value-for-money methods will be used?      |

### 5.2 Programme and project requirements

- All Government Major Projects Portfolio programmes and new measures requiring a full business case should publish an SDG contribution and trade-off statement.

- Benefits registers should include social, environmental, distributional and place-based benefits, with named owners and baselines.

- Evaluation plans should be registered at approval and findings published through the Evaluation Registry unless a lawful exemption applies.

- Procurement should use measurable social value, accessibility, fair-work, carbon, circular-economy and supply-chain criteria proportionate to the contract.

- Where several services affect one outcome, departments should pool or align funding through a single outcome agreement where law and accountability permit.

### 5.3 UK SDG Accelerator Fund

A cross-government accelerator fund should support interventions that address several goals together, such as warm homes and health, public transport and employment, nature restoration and flood resilience, or digital inclusion and skills. Its size should be set through the Spending Review. Awards should require matched outcome plans, community involvement, open evaluation and a credible route to scale; match funding should not exclude high-need places.

## 6. Data, indicators and public transparency

The ONS should lead a renewed UK SDG data partnership with the statistical authorities of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The existing UK indicator portal states that routine uploads have been paused since 2023; the delivery plan should restore a maintained, accessible and machine-readable service. [4]

| **Evidence layer**           | **Purpose**                                                                                     |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tier 1: outcomes             | A compact UK scorecard reported to Parliament; comparable over time and mapped to all 17 Goals. |
| Tier 2: drivers and delivery | Policy and service indicators used by outcome owners to diagnose change and manage delivery.    |
| Tier 3: nation and local     | Indicators selected through devolved frameworks and Local SDG Outcome Agreements.               |
| Data-gap register            | Missing, delayed or poor-quality measures, with a named owner and timetable for improvement.    |

### 6.1 Minimum data standards

- Publish definitions, sources, quality notes, revisions, geographic coverage and time lags for every headline indicator.

- Disaggregate by income, sex, age, disability, ethnicity, geography and other relevant characteristics where lawful, statistically robust and safe.

- Add rural-urban, deprivation, nation and region breakdowns; use local data where sample sizes and disclosure controls permit.

- Report both average progress and the gap between the best- and worst-served groups or places.

- Publish data in accessible HTML and open formats, with Easy Read, Welsh-language and other proportionate accessible summaries.

- Do not use algorithmic scores as a substitute for official statistics or human judgement; publish methods and bias tests for material AI-supported analysis.

### 6.2 Status rules

| **Rating**                   | **Definition**                                                                                |
|------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Green - on track             | Evidence shows the agreed 2030 trajectory is being met or the outcome is sustained at target. |
| Amber - at risk              | Progress exists but pace, coverage, funding or delivery confidence is insufficient.           |
| Red - off track              | Outcome is worsening, materially behind trajectory or lacks credible funded action.           |
| Grey - insufficient evidence | Data are missing, outdated, incomparable or not robust enough for judgement.                  |

> **NO GREEN BY AVERAGE ALONE** An indicator cannot be rated green where the national average improves but a materially disadvantaged group or place is worsening, unless the report explains the divergence and publishes a corrective plan.

## 7. Annual reporting to Parliament

The Sustainable Development Accountability Act should require a minister to lay a UK SDG Progress and Delivery Report before both Houses by 31 July each year. The Government should provide parliamentary time for debate within 60 sitting days and respond to the independent evaluation within 90 calendar days.

### 7.1 Mandatory report contents

1.  A one-page national scorecard and an assessment of each of the 17 Goals.

2.  Progress against the five outcomes, with baselines, trajectory, confidence and distributional analysis.

3.  A financial statement linking significant measures and programmes to outcomes, costs, benefits and trade-offs.

4.  A delivery statement identifying milestones met or missed, red-rated risks and named corrective actions.

5.  Voluntary nation annexes agreed with each devolved administration, plus an England and local-delivery annex.

6.  An international spillover and partnership chapter covering consumption, trade, finance, supply chains and development cooperation.

7.  A data-quality and data-gap statement signed by the responsible statistical authority.

8.  The independent evaluator's conclusions and a point-by-point government response to the previous year's recommendations.

### 7.2 Parliamentary scrutiny

- The Liaison Committee or a designated joint scrutiny mechanism should coordinate cross-cutting examination while existing select committees retain portfolio scrutiny.

- The Environmental Audit Committee, Public Accounts Committee and relevant departmental committees should be able to call outcome owners and evaluators.

- The Government should publish a recommendations tracker showing acceptance, rejection, implementation status and reasons.

- Devolved administrations should report to their own legislatures under their chosen arrangements; the UK report should not unilaterally grade devolved policy.

## 8. Independent evaluation

A new statutory UK Sustainable Development Evaluation Commission (UKSDEC) should evaluate whether the delivery system and major interventions are effective, equitable, coherent and good value. It should report to Parliament, not to the government of the day. Participation in evaluations of devolved activity should be agreed with the relevant administration and coordinated with its audit and evaluation institutions.

### 8.1 Independence safeguards

| **Safeguard**   | **Requirement**                                                                                                                                                     |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Appointment     | Open public appointments, pre-appointment parliamentary scrutiny and formal consultation with devolved legislatures.                                                |
| Composition     | Expertise in social policy, climate and nature, economics, evaluation, statistics, public finance, equalities, disability, local government and future generations. |
| Tenure          | Staggered fixed terms with transparent removal criteria and a published conflicts register.                                                                         |
| Funding         | Multi-year funding authorised through Parliament, with freedom to publish without ministerial clearance.                                                            |
| Evidence access | Proportionate statutory access to administrative data and programme records, subject to privacy, security and legal safeguards.                                     |
| Methods         | Published protocols, peer review, reproducible analysis where possible and explicit confidence judgements.                                                          |

### 8.2 Evaluation programme

| **Product**          | **Scope**                                                                                                               | **Timing**    |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------|
| Annual assessment    | Delivery system, scorecard credibility, policy coherence, funding alignment and response to previous recommendations.   | Every June    |
| Thematic evaluations | Two to four cross-goal topics selected by risk, spending, inequality and learning value.                                | Rolling       |
| Place evaluations    | Representative cities, towns, rural, coastal, island and high-deprivation areas; consent-based and improvement focused. | Annual sample |
| Mid-term evaluation  | Implementation, early outcomes, distribution, capacity, public value and changes needed for 2029-2030.                  | June 2028     |
| Final evaluation     | Contribution to outcomes, value for money, lessons and recommendations for the post-2030 framework.                     | June 2031     |

### 8.3 Evaluation methods

- Use an explicit theory of change and benefits map for each national outcome.

- Combine process, impact and value-for-money evaluation; use experimental or quasi-experimental methods where ethical and feasible.

- Apply contribution analysis and systems methods where multiple policies and external factors shape outcomes.

- Include distributional, place-based, equality, natural-capital, wellbeing and intergenerational analysis.

- Build lived experience into design, interpretation and recommendations without treating testimony as a substitute for representative evidence.

- Publish null and negative findings and register planned evaluations before results are known. [19]–[21]

### 8.4 Coordination, not duplication

UKSDEC should agree memoranda of understanding with the NAO, Audit Scotland, Audit Wales, the Northern Ireland Audit Office, the Climate Change Committee, environmental oversight bodies, statistics regulators and commissioners with relevant statutory roles. It should synthesise their findings rather than repeating specialist audits.

## 9. Participation and leave-no-one-behind assurance

Participation is a delivery control, not a communications exercise. It should identify barriers early, improve service design and show whether progress is reaching groups who are often absent from formal consultation.

| **Mechanism**         | **Minimum standard**                                                                                                                      |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| UK SDG Forum          | Annual open forum before the parliamentary report; civil society, business, unions, academia, youth and lived-experience representatives. |
| Citizens' panel       | A stratified panel reviews two cross-goal challenges each year and submits recommendations to the UK SDG Council.                         |
| Local participation   | Outcome Agreements use existing community-planning structures plus targeted outreach to excluded groups.                                  |
| Future generations    | Youth and intergenerational impact evidence are included in appraisal, evaluation and parliamentary reporting.                            |
| Accessible engagement | Reasonable adjustments, plain language, Easy Read, Welsh and proportionate language or communication support.                             |
| Feedback and redress  | Public bodies publish how evidence changed decisions and provide routes to challenge inaccurate data or exclusion.                        |

### 9.1 Leave-no-one-behind assurance

Every major outcome plan and business case should include an assurance statement answering five questions:

1.  Who is furthest behind on the baseline and why?

2.  What barriers prevent equal access, participation or benefit?

3.  How were affected groups involved in design and testing?

4.  What disaggregated indicators and safeguards will detect unequal effects?

5.  What corrective action and redress will be available if inequalities widen?

## 10. Annual delivery cycle and implementation roadmap

### 10.1 Standard annual cycle

| **Period**        | **Required activity**                                                                                     |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| August-October    | Review evidence, citizen input, evaluation findings and cross-government risks; set next-year priorities. |
| November-February | Align Budget and departmental planning; agree devolved and local outcome funding where applicable.        |
| March             | Publish updated delivery plans, named owners, milestones and evaluation registrations.                    |
| April-May         | Close the data year, quality assure indicators and publish the evidence cut for independent assessment.   |
| June              | UKSDEC publishes its independent annual assessment; government finalises corrective action.               |
| By 31 July        | Government lays the annual report before Parliament and publishes data, annexes and accessible summaries. |

### 10.2 Implementation roadmap

| **Milestone**                 | **Output**                                                                                                                                                          |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First 100 days after adoption | Publish the command paper; establish the Cabinet Committee, Secretariat and interim UK SDG Council; appoint outcome owners; launch legislation and data-gap review. |
| By 31 March 2027              | Agree the common scorecard and reporting standard; publish baseline trajectories; begin local pilots; register evaluation plans; issue Treasury appraisal guidance. |
| By 31 July 2027               | Lay the first annual report and independent baseline assessment; provide parliamentary debate and publish corrective actions.                                       |
| 2027-2028                     | Expand funded Local SDG Outcome Agreements; integrate the scorecard with departmental and nation frameworks; complete first thematic evaluations.                   |
| June-July 2028                | Publish the independent mid-term evaluation and a government acceleration plan for 2029-2030.                                                                       |
| 2029                          | Concentrate resources on red-rated outcomes and places; publish post-2030 options and begin public consultation.                                                    |
| 2030                          | Publish the UK national review, international contribution and successor commitments; preserve data continuity.                                                     |
| June 2031                     | Publish the final independent evaluation and formal government response; transfer effective mechanisms into the post-2030 framework.                                |

### 10.3 First-year success criteria

- Every five-pillar outcome has a ministerial owner, senior responsible owner, baseline, trajectory, delivery plan and registered evaluation.

- All UK departments map their priority programmes and significant spending to outcomes and disclose material trade-offs.

- The official UK SDG portal resumes routine updates and publishes a prioritised data-gap improvement plan.

- At least one diverse local pilot operates in each nation where the relevant administration agrees, with funding for analytical capacity.

- Parliament receives the first report and independent assessment on time, followed by a published corrective-action tracker.

## 11. Risk management

| **Risk**                   | **Failure mode**                                                          | **Control**                                                                                                     |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Constitutional overreach   | UK framework is perceived as directing devolved policy.                   | Consent-based agreements, non-regression, nation-owned targets and explicit reserved/devolved boundaries.       |
| Reporting without delivery | Activity is relabelled but outcomes do not improve.                       | Funded trajectories, named owners, outcome data, evaluation and corrective action for red ratings.              |
| Indicator overload         | Hundreds of measures obscure priorities and increase burden.              | Compact Tier 1 scorecard, reusable datasets, tiered evidence and published retirement rules.                    |
| Unfunded local mandates    | Councils carry new duties without capacity.                               | Funding assessment, voluntary agreements, multi-year support and reuse of existing plans and data.              |
| Uneven analytical capacity | High-need places have the least data and evaluation capability.           | Shared analytical service, accelerator funding, peer support and proportionate requirements.                    |
| Short-termism              | Annual budgets weaken prevention and long-term investment.                | Multi-year settlements, benefits ownership, long-term liability reporting and post-2030 continuity.             |
| Policy conflict            | One goal is advanced by shifting costs to another goal, place or country. | Mandatory trade-off and spillover assessment; Cabinet and Council resolution; transparent rationale.            |
| Political discontinuity    | Framework is abandoned after leadership change.                           | Statutory minimums, parliamentary ownership, independent evidence and alignment with existing national systems. |
| Data misuse or bias        | Poor-quality or AI-generated analysis harms groups or distorts decisions. | Official-statistics standards, method disclosure, human oversight, privacy and bias evaluation.                 |
| SDG-washing                | Branding creates false assurance.                                         | Additionality test, independent evaluation and prohibition on green ratings without outcome evidence.           |

### 11.1 Escalation protocol

A red-rated outcome must trigger a 90-day recovery review led by the outcome owner. The review should identify the cause, funding and capacity implications, legal or delivery barriers, alternative options and new milestones. If the Government does not accept the independent evaluator's recommended action, it must publish its evidence and rationale.

## Appendix C. Annual reporting template

Each pillar and Goal chapter should use the same compact evidence structure so Parliament and the public can compare performance over time.

| **Required field**        | **Minimum content**                                                                                      |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Outcome statement         | The public result sought by 2030 and why it matters.                                                     |
| Status                    | Green, amber, red or grey, with confidence and direction of travel.                                      |
| Headline indicators       | Baseline, latest value, 2030 target, milestone, trend and minimum disaggregation.                        |
| What changed              | Evidence of outcome change, not only activity or announcements.                                          |
| Who and where             | Distribution by income, protected groups, nation, region and local or rural-urban geography where valid. |
| Delivery                  | Milestones, expenditure, benefits, dependencies, workforce and capacity.                                 |
| Trade-offs and spillovers | Positive and negative effects on other Goals, places, generations and partner countries.                 |
| Evaluation                | Methods, findings, confidence, value for money, limitations and Evaluation Registry links.               |
| Corrective action         | Named owner, action, resource implication and due date for every red-rated issue.                        |
| Public response           | How citizen, local, devolved, civil-society and partner evidence changed decisions.                      |

> **PUBLICATION STANDARD** The report, scorecard, methods, machine-readable data, accessible summaries, independent assessment and government response should be published together. Any unavailable item must have a named owner and publication date.
