# Contributing to HESI–5P

Thank you for helping improve the HESI–5P Innovation Model. Contributions from affected communities, people with lived experience, researchers, charities, public bodies, practitioners and developers are welcome.

The shared standard is simple: be human-centred, evidence-led, sustainable, inclusive and respectful. Leave no one behind.

## Useful contributions

You can help by:

- correcting an error or outdated source;
- improving plain language or accessibility;
- testing the framework in a clearly described case study;
- proposing measurable indicators or evaluation methods;
- examining impacts across all five Ps;
- identifying excluded groups, risks or unintended consequences;
- translating material while preserving meaning and attribution; or
- improving alignment guides for the Sustainable Development Goals or responsible AI.

## Before contributing

1. Check existing issues and pull requests to avoid duplicate work.
2. Open an issue for a substantial proposal so its scope can be discussed first.
3. Keep each contribution focused on one clear change.
4. Do not include passwords, security credentials, confidential records, medical information or unnecessary personal data.
5. Submit only material that you have the right to share.

## Evidence standard

For factual, legal, scientific or statistical claims:

- use primary or official sources where available;
- link the source directly and identify its publisher;
- distinguish binding law from guidance, standards, research, forecasts and proposals;
- include the relevant date or version;
- explain important limitations, uncertainty and conflicts of interest; and
- avoid presenting correlation, aspiration or model output as proven causation.

Use [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md) as the project’s baseline source register.

## Five-P review

A substantial contribution should consider:

| Lens | Review question |
| --- | --- |
| People | Who benefits, who may be harmed and who participated? |
| Planet | What are the life-cycle effects on climate, nature, water and resources? |
| Prosperity | Does this create fair, affordable and durable value and decent work? |
| Peace | Are rights, safety, accountability, privacy and routes for challenge protected? |
| Partnership | Were affected communities and relevant disciplines meaningfully involved? |

## Accessibility checklist

Before submitting documentation:

- use plain English and explain abbreviations on first use;
- use headings in a logical order;
- give every informative image meaningful alternative text;
- provide a text alternative for complex diagrams;
- give tables clear headings and keep them simple;
- use descriptive link text rather than “click here”;
- do not use colour, position or an image alone to convey meaning; and
- check that the page remains usable on a small screen.

See [ACCESSIBILITY.md](ACCESSIBILITY.md) for the project’s accessibility approach.

## Suggested workflow

1. Open or select an issue.
2. Create a branch with a short descriptive name.
3. Make and review the focused change.
4. Open a pull request explaining what changed, why it matters, the evidence used and any unresolved limitations.
5. Respond to review comments respectfully.

Acceptance depends on evidence quality, clarity, accessibility, five-P balance and consistency with human accountability. Acceptance does not certify a contributor, organisation, product or project.

## Licence and attribution

By submitting a contribution, you confirm that you have the right to share it. Unless agreed otherwise before acceptance, documentation contributions will be made available under the repository’s [CC BY 4.0 licence](LICENSE.md).

Credit should reflect meaningful contributions. External sources must be cited; they must not be presented as project authors or endorsers.
